The problem

Three audiences, one truth about where the car is

Ride-hailing in a smaller market has to earn trust immediately: the passenger needs to know where the driver is and what they will pay, the driver needs a flow that keeps them driving instead of reading menus, and the operator needs to see the whole board.

Three interfaces reading three versions of the truth is how these products fail. So the trip — its location, fare and status — was modelled once and everything else was made a view of it.

What we built

Four decisions that shaped it.

01

The trip is the record

Location, fare, status and driver assignment are one authoritative record. Passenger app, driver app and dispatch dashboard all read the same thing, so nothing needs reconciling later.

02

Upfront fare, no surprises

Fares are quoted before the trip is accepted and locked to the record, which removes the single biggest cause of disputes in a new market.

03

A driver flow with almost no interface

The driver screen is one decision at a time, thumb-reachable, readable in daylight, and safe to use in a moving vehicle.

04

Degrades instead of dying

Weak mobile coverage is normal, not exceptional: position updates buffer and reconcile, and the interface says what it knows rather than freezing.

Architecture

How it is put together.

DATA
One canonical trip record; driver, vehicle and passenger as related entities with an auditable status history.
REAL TIME
Streaming position and status updates with buffering and reconciliation over unreliable mobile networks.
PRICING
Fare quoted, versioned and locked to the trip before acceptance.
INTERFACE
Separate passenger, driver and dispatch surfaces on one shared contract; mobile-first throughout.
OPERATIONS
Live dispatch view with vehicle verification, trip history and intervention on any active ride.
OBSERVABILITY
Every status transition logged, so any completed or cancelled trip can be replayed.

Outcomes

A live market platform, not a prototype

  • Passengers, drivers and dispatch operate from one consistent picture of every ride.
  • Fare disputes are answerable from the record instead of from memory.
  • New cities or vehicle classes are configuration on the existing model.
  • The same three people who built it still run and extend it.

Trip volume figures are the client's to share; available on request.
