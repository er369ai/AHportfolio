Company Philosophy

Eight reasons the difficult build should come here

Most of what follows is a consequence of one decision: staying small on purpose. Three people can hold an entire system in their heads, and that is what makes the hard parts tractable.

01

The same three people, schema to launch

No account layer, no rotating bench, no junior learning the domain on your budget. The person who designed your data model is the person who answers you in month nine.

02

The data model is decided first

Screens follow the model, not the reverse. That is why the fifth feature costs roughly what the second one did instead of three times as much.

03

We take the part others declined

Undocumented endpoints, a legacy database nobody wants to touch, a half-finished build with no author left. We quote on it after reading it, not before.

04

One team for backend and interface

Nothing is lost in the handoff between an agency and a dev shop, because there is no handoff. Design decisions are made with the query cost known.

05

Written down as it is built

The schema, the architecture and the reasoning behind each decision are documented as we go — so month twenty is as legible as month two, to us and to you.

06

Automation that reports honestly

Unattended work is only useful if it tells you when it fails. Retries, dead letters and an audit trail are part of the first version, not a later hardening phase.

07

AI only where it earns its place

We will tell you when a model is the wrong tool. When it is the right one, the work is context, retries, evaluation and cost — not the demo.

08

Small teams say no clearly

If your build is not right for us you will hear that in the first conversation, with a reason and, where we can, a name to talk to instead.

Evaluation & Cost
